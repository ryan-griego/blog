*Early notes on Claude Cowork from someone who's already spent months living inside OpenClaw and Hermes.*

---

A couple weeks ago I renewed my Claude Pro subscription to catch up on everything that launched since I paused it in May.

Part of what pushed me to renew was a local business owner who's adopting AI tools to sell subscription marketing services to his clients. He raved about Claude Cowork. I was deep in OpenClaw at the time and hadn't touched it.

I tested Cowork the same way I've tested OpenClaw: hand it the small stuff I'd normally do myself, like entering a row in a spreadsheet.

## Handing Off the Small Stuff

Cowork has kept the same fire going that OpenClaw lit: the itch to keep prodding and see what these desktop agents can do.

First thing I had it build: a cron job that watches my Downloads folder and sorts cover letters and resumes into the right folders while I'm applying to jobs. Claude Code could have written that same script. What Cowork adds is watching it happen. The screen tints orange and you watch Claude operate your computer directly, like a coworker instead of a chat window.

That's changed how I work. Before running through a series of steps myself, I now ask: could I just tell Claude what I'm trying to do? Give it folder access, connect the Chrome extension, let it go.

## Matching a Resume to a Job Board

Another test: I gave it my portfolio site, my resume, and a LinkedIn job search URL, then asked it to work through the results and return jobs matching my skills. It came back with a filtered list, the kind of page-by-page comparison I'd otherwise do myself across a dozen browser tabs.

## Desktop App vs. Terminal-Only Agents

Cowork is my first Anthropic-built desktop app; I've only run OpenClaw and Hermes from the terminal. It's intuitive. Connecting folders, services, and my microphone was straightforward, no config files to edit.

The feature I keep coming back to: double-tap to bring up a text or voice chat with Claude. It's there the moment you need it.

## What I Haven't Touched Yet

I still haven't tried Schedule tasks or Dispatch, and that's what excites me most about renewing. Dispatch looks like it's aiming at what OpenClaw and Hermes already do with Telegram.
